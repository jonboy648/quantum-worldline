The noes are the ones that touch the reel. If the hidden space were real, two bumps in it should tug
on each other, and the tug should fade with distance at one particular rate. The first measurement
landed almost exactly on that rate. Then it turned out that one dot on the graph was doing nearly all
the work, like a seesaw with one heavy kid on it. Drawn honestly, the line goes somewhere else. Jon
took the result back, in public.

Another idea said that time itself should show up as a certain piece of a certain number. The
computer gave exactly zero. Not small. Zero, because the magnets have a mirror symmetry that makes
the two sides cancel, like a balance with the same weight in each pan. It was never going to tip.

And some tests turned out to be quizzes where every answer gets marked correct. One of them compared
a quantity to itself under two different names. Passing a test like that means nothing, and an audit
found that most of the early passes were that kind.

So the toy is accurate, and nothing yet shows that the hidden space is more than a handy way of
drawing what the magnets do. The noes have been more interesting than the yeses.
