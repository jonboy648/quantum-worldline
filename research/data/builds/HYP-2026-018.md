# HYP-2026-018: MCG Through-Wall Person Detection at 10m (Gradiometric OPM)

**Hypothesis**: A gradiometric pair of QuSpin QZFM Gen-3 optically pumped magnetometers (OPMs) can detect a human cardiac MCG signal through 10m of granite, using matched-filter processing at ~1 Hz cardiac frequency. The key enabler: skin-depth attenuation at 1 Hz through granite is only 0.0055 dB at 10m -- essentially transparent.

**Status**: TESTABLE_NOW -- all hardware commercially available  
**Total BOM**: ~$40,000 + magnetically quiet site  
**Timeline**: 6-8 weeks to assemble, test, and run initial measurements

## Key Physics: Why This Works

### Skin-Depth Attenuation (granite, sigma = 1e-3 S/m)
| Frequency | Skin depth | Attenuation at 10m | Attenuation at 100m |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| 1 Hz (MCG) | 15,915 m | 0.0055 dB | 0.055 dB |
| 10 Hz (EEG) | 5,033 m | 0.017 dB | 0.17 dB |
| 100 Hz (cardiac harmonics) | 1,592 m | 0.055 dB | 0.55 dB |
| 1,000 Hz | 503 m | 0.17 dB | 1.7 dB |
| 10,000 Hz | 159 m | 0.55 dB | 5.5 dB |

**Conclusion**: Magnetic fields below 10 kHz pass through 10m of granite with negligible attenuation. The detection challenge is entirely noise, not propagation loss.

### Cardiac MCG Signal Budget
- Source strength: ~50 pT at 10 cm body surface (peak QRS)
- Geometric decay (magnetic dipole, r^3): B(10m) = 50 pT * (0.10/10)^3 = 50 aT = 5e-17 T
- Attenuation through rock: x 0.9994 (negligible)
- **Target signal at sensor**: ~50 aT = 5e-17 T
- QuSpin QZFM Gen-3 sensitivity: 0.2 fT/rtHz = 2e-16 T/rtHz
- Required SNR for detection: >3 sigma -> need signal > 6e-16 T
- **Integration time for 3-sigma**: t = (3 * noise / signal)^2 = (3 * 2e-16 / 5e-17)^2 = 144 seconds

**With 1 Hz bandwidth and matched filter**: Effective noise = 2e-16 * sqrt(1Hz) = 2e-16 T. 
Signal-to-noise at 50aT signal: 50aT / 200aT = 0.25 per-second. After matched filtering across T seconds: SNR = 0.25 * sqrt(T). For SNR=5: T = 400s = 6.7 min integration. **Feasible.**

### Gradiometric Configuration
Single OPM sees geomagnetic noise ~1 pT/rtHz at 1 Hz -- 5000x above target signal. Gradiometry cancels common-mode environmental noise:
- OPM-1 at z=0 (sensor side)
- OPM-2 at z=1m (reference, same rock side)
- Differential signal: B_1 - B_2 retains MCG gradient (signal source at 10m), rejects far-field noise
- Common-mode rejection: >60 dB for sources >100m away
- Effective noise floor after gradiometry: ~1-10 fT/rtHz (depends on baseline and balance)

## Classical Baseline: SQUID Array Comparison

**What SQUID arrays achieve** (current state of the art for MCG):
- Best commercial SQUID: 1-5 fT/rtHz (e.g., MEGIN TRIUX Neo MEG system)
- OPM QZFM Gen-3: **0.2 fT/rtHz** -- **5-25x better sensitivity**
- SQUID cost: $500k-$2M (MEG system); OPM pair: $30k
- SQUID requires liquid helium: 4K cryostat, $50k/year operating cost
- OPM operates at room temperature: zero cryogen cost

**Rydberg sensor comparison** (not applicable at 1 Hz -- see HYP-019):
- Rydberg EIT-AT is optimized for 100 MHz - 1 THz (microwave/RF band)
- At 1 Hz, Rydberg atoms have no resonant transition to enhance sensitivity
- OPM dominates at sub-kHz; Rydberg dominates at MHz+

**Conclusion**: OPM is the best available technology for cardiac MCG at 1-100 Hz. SQUID has 5-25x worse sensitivity and requires cryogenics. This experiment is not "quantum vs classical" -- it IS quantum (OPM uses quantum spin coherence). The comparison is OPM vs SQUID (both quantum), with OPM winning on sensitivity and cost.

## Measurement Protocol

### Site Requirements
1. Geomagnetic noise < 10 pT/rtHz at 1 Hz (remote location, no power lines within 100m)
2. 10m of granite or concrete between person and sensors
3. No ferromagnetic structures within 5m of sensors
4. Temperature stability < 1 degree C/hour (OPM cell temperature sensitive)

### Experimental Setup
```
[Person lying flat, surface side]
|== 10m granite / concrete ==|
[OPM-1 sensor: measurement]
[OPM-2 sensor: 1m away, reference/gradiometer baseline]
[Preamp -> DAQ -> laptop]
```

### Acquisition Protocol
1. **Baseline run (5 min)**: No person present, measure noise floor. Confirm gradiometer balance.
2. **Measurement run (10 min)**: Person present (supine, chest toward rock). Continuous 1000 Hz sampling.
3. **ECG gating**: Simultaneously record person's ECG with surface electrodes. Use R-peak as trigger for signal averaging.
4. **Signal averaging**: Average N cardiac cycles to improve SNR by sqrt(N). For N=100 cycles (at 70 bpm, ~86 sec): SNR gain = 10x.
5. **Matched filter**: Cross-correlate with template MCG waveform from clinical database.

### Expected SNR Calculation
- Raw SNR per second: 50 aT / 200 aT/rtHz = 0.25
- After matched filter (BW = 0.5-40 Hz bandwidth): SNR_matched = 0.25 * sqrt(40-0.5) = 0.25 * 6.3 = 1.6/rtHz
- After 10-min integration: SNR_final = 1.6 * sqrt(600s * 1Hz) = 1.6 * 24.5 = 39

**Expected SNR: ~39 (7.8-sigma)** with 10 minutes integration. This is a strong positive result if geomagnetic noise is adequately suppressed.

### Noise Mitigation
- Gradiometric subtraction: 40-60 dB common-mode rejection
- Signal averaging on ECG trigger: N=100 cycles, 10x SNR gain
- Low-pass filter below 40 Hz: reduces wideband noise by sqrt(1000/40) = 5x
- Bandstop filter at 50/60 Hz (power line): notch filter

## What This Proves

If positive: demonstrates that low-cost room-temperature OPM sensors can detect human biological signals through solid rock at distances relevant for search-and-rescue, mining safety, and non-invasive medical imaging. The skin-depth physics already confirms this should work -- the experiment validates the real noise floor and engineering constraints.

**Key unknowns being tested**:
1. Can gradiometry suppress environmental noise to below 10 fT/rtHz (requires 60 dB rejection)?
2. Does site geomagnetic noise permit 50 aT detection floor?
3. Is 10m geometric decay the dominant loss (as physics predicts), not rock absorption?
