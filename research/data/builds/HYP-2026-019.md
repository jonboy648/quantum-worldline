# HYP-2026-019: Rydberg + OPM Dual-Modality EMI Cross-Contamination Study

**Hypothesis**: When a Rydberg vapor cell sensor and an OPM are co-located, the OPM's laser fields (795nm, 1-10 mW) create a measurable EMI background in the Rydberg sensor's signal channel, and vice versa. Characterizing this cross-contamination determines the minimum sensor separation for independent operation, enabling dual-modality platforms (DC/sub-kHz OPM + RF Rydberg) in a single housing.

**Status**: TESTABLE_NOW -- all components commercially available or buildable from ECDL + vapor cell  
**Total BOM**: ~$55,000-$65,000  
**Timeline**: 3-4 months (laser procurement dominates)  
**SDR classical baseline**: RTL-SDR + calibrated horn antenna at same RF band -- see below

---

## The Physics

### Two Co-Located Sensors, Different Frequency Bands
- **OPM (QuSpin QZFM Gen-3)**: DC - 1 kHz, uses 795nm pump laser + 795nm probe at B-field-dependent Larmor frequency (~350 kHz/uT * B_earth ~ 25 MHz)
- **Rydberg EIT-AT cell**: 100 MHz - 1 THz RF sensitivity, uses 780nm probe + 480nm coupling to |n~50> Rydberg states

### Cross-Contamination Pathways
1. **OPM laser -> Rydberg**: 795nm OPM pump laser AC-Stark shifts the Rb ground state, broadening Rydberg EIT linewidth. Effect: reduced Rydberg sensitivity.
2. **Rydberg coupling laser -> OPM**: 480nm coupling photons cause photoionization of Rb in OPM cell (if leakage). Effect: increased OPM noise floor.
3. **RF local oscillator -> OPM**: Rydberg readout electronics generate 100-500 MHz RF tones that can be picked up by OPM at its Larmor frequency (if LO harmonics fall at 25 MHz).
4. **Magnetic noise from Rydberg electronics**: current-carrying coils for EIT Zeeman splitting generate B-field noise at OPM's sensitive band.

### EIT-AT Calibration Protocol
- Cesium or Rubidium 2-photon EIT: probe laser locked to D2 line (780nm), coupling laser (480nm) drives |5P> -> |nS> Rydberg state
- AT (Autler-Townes) splitting: RF field E splits EIT peak by Delta_AT = mu_dipole * E_RF / hbar
- Calibration: sweep LO frequency, measure AT splitting vs known RF field (horn antenna at calibrated distance)
- Sensitivity: demonstrated ~30 nV/m/rtHz at 104 MHz (Shaffer 2020); target < 1 uV/m/rtHz

## EIT-AT Calibration Protocol

1. **Laser lock**: Lock 780nm probe to Rb D2 (5S1/2 -> 5P3/2) using saturated absorption spectroscopy
2. **Two-photon resonance**: Scan 480nm coupling frequency to find EIT peak in transmitted probe
3. **RF calibration sweep**: Apply known E-field from calibrated horn antenna (E_ref = P_tx * G_tx / (4*pi*r^2) * eta)
4. **Measure AT splitting**: delta_AT = mu_dipole * E / hbar; for |nS> state, mu ~ n^2 * e * a0
5. **Cross-contamination measurement**: 
   - Step 1: Measure Rydberg EIT linewidth with OPM OFF -> baseline linewidth Gamma_0
   - Step 2: Turn OPM ON at separation d -> measure linewidth broadening Delta_Gamma(d)
   - Step 3: Measure OPM noise floor with Rydberg ON vs OFF -> Delta_noise_OPM(d)
   - Sweep d from 0.1m to 2m in 0.1m steps

## OPM Noise Floor vs Separation Distance (Expected)

Model: AC Stark broadening from 795nm OPM laser (power P, beam diameter w) at distance d
- Scattered light intensity at Rydberg cell: I_scatter ~ P * (w/d)^2 * reflection_factor
- For P = 1 mW, w = 1mm, d = 0.5m: I_scatter ~ 4e-9 W/cm^2 (well below EIT saturation)
- **Prediction**: OPM laser cross-contamination becomes negligible (< 1% linewidth broadening) at d > 30cm

Magnetic cross-contamination:
- OPM heater coil (I ~ 10 mA at 795nm heating): B ~ mu0*I / (2*pi*d) ~ 4e-12 T at d=0.5m
- OPM sensitivity: 0.2 fT/rtHz -- this heater noise is 20x above noise floor at d=0.5m
- **Prediction**: Must use non-magnetic heater (ITO film) or increase d > 2m for OPM independence

| Separation d | Rydberg EIT broadening | OPM noise from Rydberg | Dominant pathway |
|-------------|----------------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| 0.1m | ~50% linewidth broadening | ~100 fT/rtHz | Magnetic + optical |
| 0.3m | ~10% | ~10 fT/rtHz | Magnetic heater |
| 0.5m | ~3% | ~4 fT/rtHz | Magnetic heater |
| 1.0m | ~1% (negligible) | ~1 fT/rtHz | Near OPM spec |
| 2.0m | < 0.5% | ~0.3 fT/rtHz | Below OPM noise |

**Recommended minimum separation for dual-modality**: **1.0m with mu-metal partitioning** or **2.0m open space**

## Classical SDR Baseline

**What RTL-SDR + calibrated horn antenna provides at the same RF band (100 MHz - 3 GHz)**:

| Metric | RTL-SDR + Horn | Rydberg EIT-AT | Rydberg advantage |
|--------|---------------|---------------|------------------|
| Cost | ~$200 (RTL-SDR) + $300 (horn) = $500 | ~$40,000 system | 80x cheaper classical |
| E-field sensitivity | ~1-10 mV/m/rtHz | ~30 nV/m/rtHz (demonstrated) | **300-3000x better** |
| Frequency coverage | DC to 1.7 GHz (RTL-SDR) | ~100 MHz - 1 THz (state tunable) | Rydberg extends to THz |
| Absolute calibration | Requires antenna factor + VNA | Self-calibrating (AT splitting = SI direct) | Rydberg is SI-traceable |
| Dynamic range | ~60 dB | ~120 dB (EIT linear response) | Rydberg 2x |
| Physical size | Antenna: 30cm at 500MHz | Cell: 10mm | Rydberg 30x smaller |
| Noise figure (receiver) | 10 dB (310K noise temperature) | Quantum projection noise limited | Rydberg approaches SQL |

**Where classical SDR beats Rydberg**:
- Cost (80x cheaper for demonstration purposes)
- Portability (RTL-SDR + laptop fits in a backpack)
- Frequency agility below 100 MHz (Rydberg transitions in Rb require n>30, onset at ~90 MHz)
- Simplicity (no laser system required)

**Where Rydberg is unambiguously superior**:
- Sensitivity > 100x at resonant frequency
- SI-traceable calibration (no antenna factor uncertainty)
- Subwavelength spatial resolution (1mm cell vs 30cm antenna)
- Operation in high-EMI environments (laser-based, immune to RF receiver saturation)

**Classical baseline recommendation**: Run RTL-SDR + horn at 100 MHz and 500 MHz alongside Rydberg in same RF environment. Use classical as absolute E-field reference for AT-splitting calibration (generate known field with horn, verify Rydberg reads correct AT splitting). This cross-calibration strengthens both the Rydberg measurement and the cross-contamination study.
