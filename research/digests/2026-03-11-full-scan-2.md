### Key Paper Analysis

**PAPER-2026-020 (Composite AdS Geodesics) -- HIGHEST IMPACT FOR OUR WORK**

Bohra & Sivaramakrishnan show that timelike-separated boundary points in AdS are connected by composite timelike-spacelike geodesics, not pure spacelike ones. This is directly relevant to our holographic transport time measurement where we observe t_transport = 2.25 vs the spacelike geodesic prediction of t = 4.53 (factor-of-2 discrepancy). The composite geodesic framework suggests:

1. The correct transport path includes a timelike segment through the bulk
2. The proper time along composite geodesics can be shorter than the pure spacelike geodesic distance
3. In the BTZ case, the timelike segment probes behind the event horizon

This provides a concrete analytical framework to resolve our factor-of-2 discrepancy. The composite geodesic proper length would be approximately half the pure spacelike geodesic length, naturally explaining t_measured ~ t_predicted/2.

**PAPER-2026-023 (Superspace Worldline = QG) -- STRENGTHENS WORLDLINE-MERA BRIDGE**

Roux shows that quantum gravity, when formulated in ADM superspace, takes the form of a worldline theory. This is a deeper statement than Ashok & Murthy: it's not just that worldline path integrals CAN compute bulk propagators, but that the Hamiltonian formulation of gravity IS a worldline formalism. Key implications:

- The Keldysh partition function reduces to Euclidean 3D gravity
- Virtual graviton exchange between massive probes is computed from the cosmological boundary
- A minimal time-lapse proportional to sqrt(cosmological constant) emerges

This deepens HYP-2026-005 (MERA forces = worldline propagators) by showing the correspondence goes all the way to the quantum gravity level, not just the semiclassical geodesic approximation.

**PAPER-2026-024 (Worldline MC on Curved Space) -- FOUNDATIONAL FOR HYP-2026-007**

Corradini & Muratori (2020) developed the first numerical worldline Monte Carlo on curved spaces, specifically D-spheres and D-hyperboloids (which includes hyperbolic/AdS geometries). Their method:
- Treats curved-space effects as interaction terms
- Uses time-slicing discretization relative to proper time
- Modifies the YLOOPS algorithm with a stabilizing term
- Validated against analytical heat kernel calculations

This is exactly the methodology needed for HYP-2026-007 (worldline MC on hyperbolic lattice reproduces MERA forces). We now have a concrete algorithm to implement.

This upgrades HYP-2026-006 from "discrepancy to resolve" to "potential confirmation" -- we may have already measured the composite geodesic, not the spacelike one.

**Connection 2: Superspace Worldline Deepens MERA-Worldline Unification**

Roux (PAPER-2026-023) shows quantum gravity IS a worldline formalism in superspace. Combined with Ashok & Murthy (PAPER-2026-012), this creates a three-level correspondence:

```
Level 1: MERA circuit -> emergent bulk geometry -> hologron forces V(dr)
Level 2: Worldline path integrals on AdS -> bulk propagators G(x,x') -> forces (Ashok-Murthy)
Level 3: QG Hamiltonian in superspace IS worldline formalism -> dimensional reduction -> CFT (Roux)
```

Our MERA circuits are not just "similar to" worldline propagators -- they may be implementing the same quantum gravity calculation at the lattice level.

**Status: WATCH -- deepens theoretical understanding but no new testable prediction yet**

**Connection 3: Helios Enables Error-Protected MERA**

Quantinuum Helios (PAPER-2026-025, HW-2026-009) with 94 logical qubits via iceberg codes means we could run MERA circuits with genuine error protection for the first time. A 40-qubit MERA with error-detection codes on Helios would require ~80 physical qubits (within Helios's 98) and would give logical error rates ~1e-4, which is far better than raw hardware.

This makes HYP-2026-002 substantially more feasible and more scientifically meaningful. A holographic force measurement on error-protected logical qubits would be the first such measurement where hardware noise is demonstrably sub-dominant.
