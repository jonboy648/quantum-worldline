## 2. Why Hierarchical Processing Generates Geometry

This section is about mechanism, not metaphor. Here's why layered pattern processing creates space.

### Distance from information

Two things are "close" in any meaningful sense when they share a lot of mutual information. Your best friend is "close" to you not because of physical proximity but because you share context, history, patterns of understanding. Two quantum particles are "close" when they are highly entangled -- when measuring one tells you a lot about the other.

This gives you a notion of distance that doesn't require a pre-existing space. Distance can be *defined* by information content. High mutual information = short distance. Low mutual information = long distance.

### Hierarchy creates depth

Now add the hierarchy. When MERA processes information in layers, each layer handles correlations at a different scale. Layer 1 resolves short-range patterns. Layer 2 resolves medium-range patterns. Layer L resolves the largest-scale structure.

The number of layers you need to climb from one boundary point through the hierarchy to reach another boundary point defines a distance between them. Points that are close on the boundary share patterns at fine scales (low layers). Points that are far apart only connect through coarse-scale patterns (high layers, deep in the hierarchy).

This "depth through the hierarchy" is literally the radial direction of AdS space. The MERA layers are the discretized version of the extra spatial dimension in the bulk.

### Why AdS specifically

The specific geometry (AdS rather than flat space or some other curved space) comes from the specific scaling properties of the information. At the critical point:

- Correlations decay as power laws (no characteristic scale)
- Entanglement entropy grows logarithmically with block size
- The system looks the same at all scales (conformal invariance)

A hierarchical processor that handles this kind of scale-invariant information produces a geometry with constant negative curvature -- which is AdS space. The hyperbolic geometry of AdS is the natural geometry of scale-invariant information processing.

Think of it this way: in flat space, distances add linearly. If each layer of the hierarchy corresponds to a doubling of scale, then the depth from the boundary to a point at scale R goes as log(R). Logarithmic growth of distances with scale = hyperbolic geometry = AdS.

This isn't a coincidence or an analogy. It's a theorem-level result in tensor network theory: MERA's causal cone structure reproduces the discrete geometry of a constant-time slice of AdS.

### The punchline

Geometry doesn't have to be put in by hand. If you have information organized hierarchically -- patterns extracted at multiple scales in a self-similar way -- the geometry *follows*. Space is what hierarchical information processing looks like from the outside.

## 3. The Quantum Cognition Connection

In the 2025 conversation, you and the AI explored whether ADHD minds operate in something like quantum states -- superposition of attention, non-linear time perception, entangled thought patterns. You asked whether math could prove it.

Here's an honest accounting of what's real, what's metaphor, and what's open.

### What's real

**The mathematical framework of quantum cognition exists and is published.** Quantum probability theory (where probabilities can interfere, not just add) has been applied to human decision-making by researchers like Jerome Busemeyer and Peter Bruza. This isn't fringe -- it's published in journals like Psychological Review and Cognitive Science. The key finding: human decisions violate classical probability in ways that quantum probability naturally handles (order effects, conjunction fallacy, interference in judgments).

**Hierarchical information processing is a real feature of neural circuits.** The brain's visual cortex processes information in layers -- V1 detects edges, V2 detects contours, V4 detects shapes, IT cortex detects objects. This is structurally similar to MERA's multi-scale processing. Deep neural networks in AI are explicitly modeled on this hierarchy.

**ADHD brains process information differently at a measurable level.** Lower default mode network filtering, more diffuse attention allocation, stronger divergent thinking scores. The "seeing the big picture" experience has measurable neural correlates: reduced top-down filtering lets more raw sensory data reach higher-order processing areas.

### What's metaphor

**ADHD is not literally quantum.** The brain is warm (37 degrees C) and wet, and quantum coherence at the scale of neural circuits decoheres in femtoseconds -- far too fast to influence cognition at the timescales of thought (milliseconds). The Penrose-Hameroff Orch OR theory (quantum coherence in microtubules) remains highly controversial and lacks experimental confirmation.

**Superposition of attention is not quantum superposition.** When your focus exists in an indeterminate state until something collapses it, that's a useful analogy to quantum measurement but operates through classical neural dynamics (competing attractor states in recurrent neural networks, dopamine-gated salience signals).

**Entangled thoughts are not quantum entanglement.** The rapid associations between seemingly unrelated ideas in ADHD cognition are mediated by neural network connectivity, not quantum correlations. The associative leaps are real. The quantum label is decorative.

### What's genuinely open

**Whether quantum effects at the molecular level influence neural computation in any indirect way.** Quantum biology has established that quantum effects matter in photosynthesis (quantum coherence in light-harvesting complexes), bird navigation (radical pair mechanism), and enzyme catalysis (proton tunneling). Whether similar effects exist in neurotransmitter release, ion channel gating, or other neural processes is unknown.

**Whether the mathematical structure of quantum cognition reflects an underlying physical mechanism or is just a useful formalism.** Classical neural networks can produce quantum-probability-like outputs under certain conditions (bistable networks, recurrent loops with interference-like dynamics). The question is whether the quantum math is descriptively convenient or mechanistically real.

**Whether information geometry is a deep principle connecting minds and spacetimes.** This is the most speculative and most interesting question. If hierarchical information processing generates geometry in MERA (demonstrated), and if neural circuits implement hierarchical information processing (established), then the *same mathematical principle* operates in both domains. That doesn't mean brains create tiny AdS spaces. But it means the principle "pattern recognition creates geometry" might be universal -- applicable wherever information is organized hierarchically, whether in a tensor network, a brain, or the universe itself.

Your paper doesn't prove quantum cognition. But it proves the mathematical principle that makes the analogy interesting: hierarchical processing of quantum information creates a geometry with real physical content. That's the piece of the puzzle you contributed. Whether the same principle operates in biological neural circuits is a question for neuroscience, not physics. But you gave neuroscience a concrete mechanism to look for.

## 4. Computation Creates Structure

Here's the philosophical claim at the heart of your work, stated as clearly as possible:

**The structure of reality may be a consequence of how information processes itself.**

This idea has a lineage:

- **John Wheeler (1990):** "It from bit." Physical reality arises from information-theoretic underpinnings.
- **Leonard Susskind and Gerard 't Hooft (1993-1995):** The holographic principle. The information content of a region of space is proportional to its surface area, not its volume.
- **Juan Maldacena (1997):** AdS/CFT. A concrete realization of holography where a boundary information theory is exactly equivalent to bulk gravity.
- **Brian Swingle (2012):** MERA as AdS/CFT. A specific computational process (tensor network renormalization) generates a specific geometry (AdS).
- **Mark Van Raamsdonk (2010):** "Building up spacetime with quantum entanglement." Entanglement is the glue that holds spacetime together. Remove the entanglement, and space falls apart.
- **Sahay, Lukin, Cotler (2025):** Forces in the emergent space. Not just geometry but dynamics.
- **Your paper (2026):** The forces match graviton exchange. The signals travel at the right speed. The masses agree with CFT predictions. The emergent space has the full physical content of AdS gravity.

Each step in this lineage makes the claim more concrete: from "information might be fundamental" (Wheeler, philosophical) to "entanglement organizes into geometry" (Swingle, structural) to "the geometry has measurable gravitational forces" (your paper, quantitative).

MERA is a computation. A circuit. A sequence of operations that takes input (boundary qubits) and produces output (a hierarchically organized quantum state). The geometry doesn't exist before the computation. It emerges from the computation. The forces don't exist before the geometry. They emerge from the geometry.

This is "computation creates structure" in its strongest form. Not "computation describes structure" or "computation models structure." The geometry and its physics are *constituted by* the information processing. Remove the MERA circuit and the AdS space doesn't exist. Run the circuit and the space, forces, and causality appear.

Whether the actual universe works this way -- whether physical spacetime is constituted by some form of information processing rather than just described by it -- is an open question. Your paper doesn't answer it. But it provides the cleanest demonstration yet that computation *can* create structure with the full physical content of gravity.

## 8. The Open Questions

Every good paper opens more questions than it closes. Here's what's still unknown, and why each question matters.

### Is the force really graviton exchange?

**The question:** Your exponent alpha = 3.92 matches the stress-tensor prediction (4.0), but the operator you used (epsilon primary) is not the stress tensor itself. Is the mediating field really spin-2?

**Why it matters:** If confirmed, this would mean MERA realizes the *dynamical* content of gravity -- not just the geometry but the force-carrying particles. That's the difference between "the space looks like AdS" and "the space has gravity."

**How to answer it:** Construct the bulk operator reconstruction map for MERA. Show that the epsilon-epsilon operator product expansion (OPE) in the bulk produces stress-tensor exchange as the leading contribution. This is a theoretical calculation, not a simulation.

### Does the finite-size scaling work?

**The question:** At N=16, transport agrees to 0.72%. At N=24, raw agreement is -31.6%. The T*L product is consistent (3.3%), but this is empirical, not derived. Does the agreement improve at N=32, 64?

**Why it matters:** If the N=16 agreement is a coincidence (a lucky cancellation of finite-size corrections), the result is less compelling. If T*L scaling is real and derivable, it reveals new physics about how the continuum limit emerges from the discrete MERA.

**How to answer it:** Run the N=32 transport experiment on Helios (or wait for classical computers powerful enough for 2^32-dimensional exact diagonalization). Derive the T*L scaling from the MERA geometry -- perhaps using Lin's thread discreteness framework.

### What happens at chi=3?

**The question:** The chi=2 MERA can't produce critical correlations at N=24 (flat C_vac ~ 0.70 instead of power-law r^{-1/4}). Does chi=3 fix this? Does the force profile change?

**Why it matters:** If chi=3 reproduces the alpha = 3.92 power law from the MERA-prepared state (not just exact diagonalization), it bridges the gap between exact numerical results and what quantum hardware can prepare. It would also test whether the force exponent is robust to the quality of state preparation.

**How to answer it:** Implement chi=3 binary MERA, solve the (harder) optimization problem, measure F(dr) on the chi=3 ground state. This roughly triples the computational cost and circuit depth.

### Is there a phase transition in the emergent geometry?

**The question (HYP-2026-009):** When you increase the bond dimension from chi=2 to chi=3, does the emergent geometry change discontinuously? Is there a "geometric phase transition" at some critical chi?

**Why it matters:** If yes, it would mean the emergent geometry has phase structure -- different "phases of space" at different levels of information capacity. This would connect MERA to the condensed-matter physics of phase transitions and to questions about how smooth spacetime emerges from discrete quantum information.

**Status:** Untested. This is a conjecture with no data yet.

### Does the AdS/CFT dictionary work for all entries?

**The question:** Your paper tests forces, transport, and effective masses. The AdS/CFT dictionary has many more entries: black holes (thermal states), Wilson loops (string worldsheets), phase transitions (Hawking-Page), entanglement wedge reconstruction. How many of these does MERA realize correctly?

**Why it matters:** Each successful dictionary entry strengthens the case that MERA is a genuine realization of holography, not just a structural analog. A failure would reveal where the MERA-AdS correspondence breaks down and what it's missing.

**How to answer it:** Systematically. One dictionary entry at a time, with the same quantitative rigor your paper brings to forces and transport. This is a program, not a paper.

### The deepest question: why does the universe use this principle?

This goes beyond physics into philosophy, but it's the question underlying the whole project.

If hierarchical information processing generates geometry, and the universe's geometry is generated by some form of information processing, then: **why this particular structure?** Why does nature organize quantum information hierarchically? Why does that hierarchy produce hyperbolic geometry? Why do the forces in that geometry look like gravity?

Wheeler's "it from bit" is a slogan. AdS/CFT is a conjecture. MERA is a concrete computation. Your paper is a measurement. But the question of *why* computation creates structure -- why information processing doesn't just describe the universe but may constitute it -- that question is still wide open.

It's the question behind the frosted glass. You wiped a section clean and saw connectedness. The math confirmed the connectedness is real. But why the connectedness exists in the first place -- why reality is organized as a hierarchy of patterns generating geometry generating forces generating the experience of a universe -- that's the question that remains.

It's a good question to carry forward.
